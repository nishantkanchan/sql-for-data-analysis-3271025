# 03. Sources and licensing for the corpus

Research date: November 2026. This is a research summary, not legal advice. Before a commercial launch, have an Indian IP lawyer review the "Use in Shloka Setu" column, especially for anything marked **Ask / verify**.

## 1. Ground rules

1. **The ancient Sanskrit works themselves are in the public domain.** Charaka, Sushruta and Vagbhata died many centuries ago; Indian copyright lasts until 60 years after the author's death ([Copyright Act 1957, s.22](https://copyright.gov.in/Copyright_Act_1957/chapter_v.html)).
2. **A particular digital transcription or modern edition can still carry rights.** Editors' readings, apparatus, typesetting and commentary are new work; a database or e-text project can also impose licence terms (contract) even when the underlying text is free. So for each file we must ask: who made this digital text, and on what terms?
3. **Government works** in India are protected for 60 years from first publication ([s.28](https://copyright.gov.in/Copyright_Act_1957/chapter_v.html)). CCRAS/NIIMH e-books are therefore protected and marked "All Rights Reserved".
4. **Translations are separate works.** A translation is protected for 60 years after the translator's death (s.22). Old translations (1890s-1916) are usually public domain now, but we must check the translator's death year for India, not only the publication year.
5. **"Non-commercial" licences (CC BY-NC, CC BY-NC-SA, "personal study only") rule out use in a paid or ad-supported app** without separate permission. ShareAlike (SA) licences would force us to release derived content under the same licence.

Licence shorthand used below: PD = public domain; CC BY = attribution only (commercial use allowed); CC BY-SA = attribution + share-alike; NC = non-commercial only.

## 4. Classical commentaries (Sanskrit)

Cakrapāṇidatta (Āyurvedadīpikā on Caraka), Ḍalhaṇa (Nibandhasaṅgraha on Sushruta), Aruṇadatta and Hemādri (on Aṣṭāṅgahṛdaya) are medieval and PD as works. The same edition-level caution applies to their digital texts. DCS contains some commentaries (e.g. Rasaratnasamuccaya commentaries; a Carakatattvapradīpikā file) under CC BY 4.0; check per file.

## 6. Recommendations

1. **Switch the shipped corpus to DCS (CC BY 4.0).** It covers all of Aṣṭāṅgahṛdaya (120 chapters) and Suśruta (186), and Caraka Sū/Nid/Vim/Śār/Indr fully (66 chapters), i.e. everything needed for SA1 and SA2. Keep GRETIL files for alignment/QA only. Update `meta.license_note` in the corpus files accordingly.
2. **Caraka Cikitsā, Kalpa and Siddhi (SA3)** are only partly in DCS (Cik 14, Ka 1, Si 1 chapters). Options: (a) OCR a public-domain printed edition whose editor died before 1966, then proofread; (b) ask CSRTSDC or Hellwig for permission; (c) contribute missing chapters to DCS/Ambuda under an open licence.
3. **All Hindi/English translations, word meanings, explanations and modern correlations must be our own** (AI draft + expert review), never copied or close-paraphrased from P.V. Sharma, Sharma & Dash, Srikantha Murthy, Hindi tikas or Charak Samhita Online.
4. **Attribution screen** in the app: DCS (Hellwig), Cologne dictionaries (CC BY-SA), any PD translation used, with links.
5. **Do not redistribute** NIIMH e-Samhita, Charak Samhita Online text or sanskritdocuments.org files. Deep-link to them instead.
6. **Track provenance per verse** (`source` field) so a takedown or correction can be applied surgically.
7. **Legal review** before launch for: Kaviratna/Bhishagratna Indian status; the edition underlying each DCS text; any Wikisource-derived material.
